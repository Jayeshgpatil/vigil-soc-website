About

Vigilance, as an operating discipline

VGIL exists because most organisations discover an intrusion from a third party — a customer, a journalist, or the attacker's own ransom note. We think that is a solvable problem, and that solving it should not require building a security department.

Why we built this

Our founding team spent a decade responding to incidents at organisations that had already bought good tools. The tooling was rarely the problem. The problem was that at 03:00 on a Sunday, the alert that mattered landed in a queue nobody was reading.

Detection without an operation behind it is theatre. VGIL is built the other way round: the operation comes first, the technology exists to make the analysts faster, and the measure of success is how quickly a real attack is stopped — not how many alerts the platform produced.

Operating principles

Say what you actually detect

Our rule catalog is published. If a technique is not covered, we say so rather than hiding behind "advanced AI".

The clock is the metric

Time to detect, time to acknowledge, time to contain. Alert counts and dashboard density measure nothing worth measuring.

Never act outside the runbook

An unauthorised containment action that breaks a production system is our failure, not a necessary cost of speed.

Blameless, but not consequence-free

Every miss gets written up and every write-up changes something concrete — a rule, a runbook, a rota.

Your telemetry is yours

We do not sell it, share it, or train models on it. You can export all of it, in open formats, on any day of the contract.

The operation

How the SOC is actually staffed

Three regional centres on an eight-hour handover cycle. Nobody works a night shift, so nobody triages your critical alert at 04:00 on their fourth consecutive night.

UTC+0 · DUBLIN

Europe centre

Covers 06:00–14:00 UTC. Primary for EMEA customers and the compliance engineering team. 22 analysts across Tier 1–3.

UTC−5 · TORONTO

Americas centre

Covers 14:00–22:00 UTC. Home of the incident response practice and the forensics lab. 24 analysts and 6 responders.

UTC+8 · SINGAPORE

APAC centre

Covers 22:00–06:00 UTC. Runs the threat intelligence team and overnight hunting programme. 18 analysts and 4 intel researchers.

Handover discipline

Every shift ends with a live handover call, not a written note. Open incidents are walked through case by case, with the incoming shift lead repeating back the state of each one before the outgoing lead signs off.

Any incident older than one shift gets a named owner who follows it across handovers, so nothing becomes everybody's problem and therefore nobody's.
